Credit approval prediction with a stacked ensemble

Honours research project

  • Built a stacked ensemble classifier that predicts credit approval, combining Logistic Regression, Random Forest and a Support Vector Machine.
  • Handled class imbalance with imbalanced-learn and compared the stacked results against the individual models.
  • On the held-out test set the stacked ensemble reached the highest accuracy, 92.33%. Random Forest was marginally ahead on F1 (82.73% against 82.00%) and ROC AUC (97.29% against 97.25%), so the two were very close, and both beat Logistic Regression and the SVM.
  • The repository contains the stacking results and the trained meta-model, which is stored in parts because of GitHub's 100 MB file limit.
  • 92.33%Accuracy, stacked ensemble
  • 82.73%F1 score, Random Forest
  • 97.29%ROC AUC, Random Forest

To use the trained model, download the three parts from the repository and join them with this command:

cat best_meta_model.pkl.part* > best_meta_model.pkl

Evaluating differential privacy for Sepedi text classification

NLP research

  • Ran what I believe to be the first empirical study of differential privacy on Sepedi, a low-resource South African language.
  • Compared Logistic Regression, Random Forest and Gaussian Naïve Bayes under privacy constraints.
  • Measured the trade-off between privacy and accuracy across four privacy budgets and found Logistic Regression at ε = 5.0 gave the best balance.
  • Wrote practical recommendations for POPIA-compliant NLP systems for South African languages.
